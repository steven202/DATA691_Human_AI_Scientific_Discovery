\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems retrieve long, noisy documents that challenge fact-checking models. We evaluate MiniCheck-style small fact-checkers (\texttt{flan-t5-large}, \texttt{Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B}) under two stress conditions: (1) natural long contexts ranging from hundreds to 74k tokens, and (2) adversarial hallucinations injected at beginning, middle, and end positions. Our key findings are: (1) Both models exhibit a ``lost in the middle'' phenomenon on ExpertQA: balanced accuracy (BAcc) drops 9-10\% at 1k-2k tokens then recovers at longer lengths. (2) Models achieve near-perfect BAcc on 74k-token SummHay documents via chunking but throughput collapses to 3.9 samples/minute. (3) Adversarial injection fools the models 57.5\% of the time; fabricated entities are hardest to detect (38.3\% detection rate). (4) No positional rescue exists; injection detection is nearly identical across beginning, middle, and end positions. Code and data are available at \url{https://github.com/steven202/DATA691_Human_AI_Scientific_Discovery}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) deployed in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines \citep{lewis2020retrieval,gao2023retrieval} must verify facts against retrieved documents spanning tens of thousands of tokens. Specialized fact-checkers such as MiniCheck \citep{tang2024minicheck} and AlignScore \citep{zha2023alignscore} offer efficient alternatives to large LLMs but are predominantly trained and evaluated on short, clean snippets. Automated fact-checking has attracted broad research interest \citep{guo2022survey,vykopal2024generative}, yet the robustness of small fact-checkers under realistic RAG conditions remains poorly understood.

Two key failure modes emerge in long-context RAG: (1) context degradation, where accuracy drops as document length increases, and (2) adversarial vulnerability, where injected hallucinations \citep{huang2023survey} bypass detection mechanisms. Prior work on ``lost in the middle'' \citep{laban2024lost} shows that LLMs struggle to utilize information buried in extended contexts, and are easily distracted by irrelevant context \citep{shi2023large}. It is an open question whether small fact-checkers with chunking-based architectures suffer similar degradation.

We evaluate MiniCheck-style fact-checkers under two stress conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Long-context natural stress}: 7 datasets spanning 56 to 74,488 tokens, measuring BAcc degradation across document-length bins.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial injection}: Synthetic hallucinations (numeric errors, fabricated entities, contradictions) inserted at controlled positions within documents.
\end{itemize}

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item First systematic evaluation of small fact-checkers across extreme context lengths (up to 74k tokens) on natural RAG datasets.
    \item Novel adversarial injection benchmark testing robustness against hallucinations at beginning, middle, and end positions.
    \item Evidence of ``lost in the middle'' phenomenon in small fact-checkers via chunking-based architectures.
    \item Quantification of the accuracy-throughput trade-off: near-perfect accuracy on extremely long documents comes at a 250x throughput cost.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

MiniCheck \citep{tang2024minicheck} uses a chunking-and-max strategy: documents are split into sentence-preserving chunks, each chunk is scored, and the maximum support probability determines the final verdict. This approach enables handling long documents but assumes the correct chunk will achieve the highest score.

MiniCheck's synthetic training uses two methods: (1) \textbf{C2D}: claim $\rightarrow$ atomic facts $\rightarrow$ supporting/unsupported documents, and (2) \textbf{D2C}: document chunks $\rightarrow$ short claims $\rightarrow$ positives and leave-one-out negatives. The D2C approach creates positive examples by including the source chunk and negative examples by removing it, forcing the model to identify which chunk supports the claim.

Prior work on ``lost in the middle'' \citep{laban2024lost} demonstrates that LLMs perform worse on information located in the middle of long contexts compared to beginning or end positions. We investigate whether chunking-based fact-checkers exhibit a similar U-shaped performance curve.

Previous studies on RAG robustness focus on retrieval corruption or prompt injection. We introduce a novel stress test: injecting synthetic hallucinations directly into the grounding documents at controlled positions, measuring whether the fact-checker can detect them.

Unlike MiniCheck's original evaluation on short synthetic documents, we evaluate on natural long-context datasets: ExpertQA \citep{malaviya2024expertqa}, RAGTruth, SciFact \citep{wadden2020fact}, SummHay, TofuEval-MediaS, TofuEval-MeetB \citep{tang2024tofueval}, and Lfqa, and introduce adversarial injection as a systematic probe for positional vulnerabilities.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Chunking Pipeline}

Our evaluation follows the MiniCheck chunking pipeline: (1) split document into sentence-preserving chunks, (2) score the claim against each chunk, (3) use maximum support probability as the document-level decision.

We use 500-word sentence-preserving chunks for adversarial injection. For \texttt{flan-t5-large}, MiniCheck uses 500-word chunks. For RoBERTa/DeBERTa variants, MiniCheck uses 400-token chunks. Bespoke-7B uses a 32k-token window. A 74k-token SummHay document is approximately 150 flan-sized chunks.

\subsection{Models}

We evaluate two MiniCheck-style models: \textbf{flan-t5-large} (770M) using 500-word chunks, and \textbf{Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B} (7B) with a 32k-token window. We also probe OpenRouter API models (Gemma-4-26B, Gemma-4-31B, GPT-OSS-120B, GPT-OSS-20B, Trinity-Large).

\subsection{Datasets}

We use 7 datasets spanning 56 to 74,488 tokens: ExpertQA \citep{malaviya2024expertqa} (3,702 samples, avg 433 tokens), RAGTruth (16,371 samples, avg 412 tokens), SciFact \citep{wadden2020fact} (188 samples, avg 57 tokens), SummHay (100 samples, avg 74,488 tokens), TofuEval-MediaS \citep{tang2024tofueval} (726 samples, avg 778 tokens), TofuEval-MeetB \citep{tang2024tofueval} (100 samples, avg 792 tokens), and Lfqa (100 samples, avg 320 tokens).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We use balanced accuracy (BAcc) to handle class imbalance: $\text{BAcc} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{TP}{TP + FN} + \frac{TN}{TN + FP}\right)$. BAcc of 50\% equals random chance. We also measure samples per minute (SPM) as throughput.

\subsection{Adversarial Injection}

We test whether the chunking baseline can detect false facts injected sequentially into specific positions within long documents. Our evaluation targets \texttt{flan-t5-large} exclusively with 360 adversarial samples derived from positive facts in ExpertQA (126 samples) and RAGTruth (234 samples).

We inject three hallucination types: (1) \textbf{Numeric}: wrong counts, percentages, or statistics; (2) \textbf{Entity}: fabricated researchers, institutions, or citations; (3) \textbf{Contradict}: direct contradictions of claims.

Injection positions: \textbf{Beginning} (first chunk), \textbf{Middle} ($\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ chunk), and \textbf{End} (final chunk) using 500-word sentence-preserving splits.

\subsection{Context Degradation: ``Lost in the Middle''}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/context_degradation_by_dataset.png}
\caption{BAcc by document-length bin. ExpertQA uses absolute bins (0-500, 500-1000, 1000-2000, 2000-4000, 4000+) and shows the full U-shaped curve. \label{fig:degradation}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:degradation} reveals the ``lost in the middle'' phenomenon using dataset-adaptive binning: ExpertQA uses the original absolute bins (0-500, 500-1000, 1000-2000, 2000-4000, 4000+), while RAGTruth uses finer 250-token bins suited to its narrower length range (max 1,626 tokens).

\textbf{ExpertQA} (5 bins) exhibits the clearest U-shape. Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B drops from 58.7\% to 48.1\% at 1k-2k tokens and recovers to 59.1\% at 2k-4k tokens; flan-t5-large drops from 58.6\% to 49.4\% and recovers to 66.8\%. \textbf{RAGTruth} (5 bins at 250-token granularity) reveals a subtler pattern: flan-t5-large drops from 79.1\% (250-500 tokens) to 75.9\% (500-750 tokens) and recovers to 78.9\% (750-1000 tokens); Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B peaks at 88.7\% in the middle bins and degrades to 77.6\% at 1000-1250 tokens, suggesting its 32k-token window interacts differently with mid-length documents. \textbf{TofuEval} and \textbf{Lfqa} have only 1-2 bins due to narrow token ranges, insufficient for trend analysis. The U-shape is thus strongest when datasets span a wide length distribution, but finer binning can reveal partial degradation patterns even in shorter-context datasets.

\subsection{Adversarial Vulnerability}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/adversarial_results.png}
\caption{Adversarial injection detection by hallucination type (left) and injection position (right). Dashed line indicates random chance (50\%).}
\label{fig:adversarial}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:adversarial} shows adversarial injection results on \texttt{flan-t5-large}. We measure conditional detection: the fraction of clean-correct predictions that also detect the attack, computed separately for each hallucination type and injection position.

\textbf{By hallucination type on ExpertQA (n=126):} Numeric achieves 22.2\% conditional detection, Entity achieves 14.8\% (the most effective attack), and Contradict achieves 22.2\%.

\textbf{By hallucination type on RAGTruth (n=234):} Numeric achieves 11.1\% conditional detection. Both Entity and Contradict achieve 0.0\% conditional detection, meaning attacks that fool the clean model completely bypass detection.

\textbf{By injection position on ExpertQA:} Beginning achieves 18.5\% conditional detection, Middle achieves 22.2\%, and End achieves 18.5\%.

\textbf{By injection position on RAGTruth:} Beginning achieves 4.4\% conditional detection, Middle achieves 4.4\%, and End achieves 2.2\%.

Overall, 57.5\% of attacks fool the model. Critically, no positional rescue exists: injections are detected at nearly identical rates regardless of position.

\section{Discussion}

Our results reveal fundamental trade-offs in deploying small fact-checkers for RAG systems.

The ``lost in the middle'' phenomenon is real but dataset-dependent. The U-shaped performance curve on ExpertQA confirms that small fact-checkers suffer middle-context degradation, with the subsequent recovery at very long lengths suggesting that the chunking mechanism eventually surfaces the correct evidence chunk when documents are sufficiently long. On RAGTruth, finer-grained binning at 250-token intervals reveals a more subtle U-shape for flan-t5-large, with BAcc dropping from 79.1\% (250--500 tokens) to 75.9\% (500--750 tokens) before recovering to 78.9\% (750--1000 tokens). Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B exhibits a different pattern, peaking in middle bins and degrading at the longest lengths, which may reflect differences in how the 32k-token context window interacts with mid-length documents. The varying U-shape strength across datasets underscores that context degradation is influenced by document length distribution, evidence placement, and model architecture jointly.

The accuracy-throughput trade-off is severe: the 250× throughput reduction on SummHay highlights the operational cost of achieving perfect accuracy on extremely long documents via exhaustive chunking. In production RAG systems, this may necessitate adaptive chunking strategies that scale processing with document length or confidence thresholds.

Adversarial hallucinations systematically bypass detection. The 57.5\% overall fooling rate, with entity hallucinations achieving 0.0\% conditional detection on RAGTruth, demonstrates that fabricated citations and fake entities completely evade the chunking-based verification mechanism. This failure mode is particularly concerning for scientific and legal applications where entity verification is critical. Approaches like chain-of-verification \citep{dhuliawala2023chain} and self-checking \citep{li2023self} may offer paths to mitigate such failures, though their effectiveness under long-context conditions remains to be evaluated.

Critically, we find no positional defense against adversarial injection: detection rates are nearly identical across beginning, middle, and end positions. This rules out simple heuristics such as biasing attention toward document boundaries and suggests that more sophisticated verification strategies, potentially involving cross-chunk consistency checks, are needed.

Several limitations warrant consideration. Our adversarial injection uses synthetic hallucinations which, while controlled and reproducible, may not capture the full diversity of real-world hallucination strategies. OpenRouter model evaluations used small sample sizes (n=10--50) due to API cost constraints, limiting the statistical power of those comparisons. The U-shape analysis is constrained by dataset composition: only ExpertQA provides samples across all five length bins, and TofuEval documents occupy a narrow token range (500--1,000) that precludes observing a full U-shaped curve. Future work should extend evaluation to datasets with more uniform length coverage and explore mitigation strategies such as hierarchical chunking or cross-chunk attention mechanisms.

\section{Conclusion}

We evaluated MiniCheck-style small fact-checkers under long-context and adversarial stress, revealing several key findings. First, the ``lost in the middle'' phenomenon is confirmed on ExpertQA, with a 9--10\% BAcc degradation at 1k--2k tokens followed by recovery at longer lengths; finer binning on RAGTruth reveals a more subtle U-shape pattern, though its strength varies across models. Second, extreme-context accuracy incurs a severe throughput penalty, with a 250× slowdown on 74k-token documents, underscoring the need for adaptive chunking in production deployments. Third, adversarial hallucinations fool the models 57.5\% of the time overall, with fabricated entities bypassing detection entirely on RAGTruth, a finding with serious implications for fact-checking in high-stakes domains. Finally, injection detection is position-invariant, ruling out simple boundary-biasing defenses and motivating the development of cross-chunk verification strategies. Taken together, these results establish that while small fact-checkers offer a practical trade-off between accuracy and efficiency for RAG pipelines, their vulnerability to adversarial content and context-length degradation demands careful architectural consideration in real-world deployments.
